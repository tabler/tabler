## Fill colors

Fills are background tints for controls and small surfaces. There are two kinds.

### Neutral fills

The neutral fills are the text color over a transparent background, so the same fill reads the same on the page, a card, a dropdown or a modal, in both color modes. Use them from the strongest to the faintest:

- `--tblr-fill-primary` (16%) for the inactive track of a slider and other strong neutral shapes. `--tblr-fill` is an alias for it.
- `--tblr-fill-secondary` (8%) for progress tracks and the hover state of neutral badges.
- `--tblr-fill-tertiary` (4% in light mode, 6% in dark) for neutral badges, avatars, code and the hover state of quiet controls. `--tblr-hover-bg` points at it.
- `--tblr-fill-quaternary` (2%) for faint section backgrounds.

<Example>
<div class="row g-3">
  <div class="col-6 col-md-3"><div class="bg-fill-primary p-4 rounded">bg-fill-primary</div></div>
  <div class="col-6 col-md-3"><div class="bg-fill-secondary p-4 rounded">bg-fill-secondary</div></div>
  <div class="col-6 col-md-3"><div class="bg-fill-tertiary p-4 rounded">bg-fill-tertiary</div></div>
  <div class="col-6 col-md-3"><div class="bg-fill-quaternary p-4 rounded">bg-fill-quaternary</div></div>
</div>
</Example>

### Color fills

Every base color has three opaque fills: `--tblr-{color}-fill` for the resting state, and `--tblr-{color}-fill-hover` and `--tblr-{color}-fill-active` for the interactive states. Colored alerts and statuses use them. Each has a `bg-{color}-fill`, `bg-{color}-fill-hover` and `bg-{color}-fill-active` utility.

Unlike the `-lt` shades, the fills are opaque, so they look the same on any surface.

<Example>
<div class="row g-3">
  <div class="col-4"><div class="bg-blue-fill text-blue p-4 rounded">bg-blue-fill</div></div>
  <div class="col-4"><div class="bg-blue-fill-hover text-blue p-4 rounded">bg-blue-fill-hover</div></div>
  <div class="col-4"><div class="bg-blue-fill-active text-blue p-4 rounded">bg-blue-fill-active</div></div>
  <div class="col-4"><div class="bg-green-fill text-green p-4 rounded">bg-green-fill</div></div>
  <div class="col-4"><div class="bg-orange-fill text-orange p-4 rounded">bg-orange-fill</div></div>
  <div class="col-4"><div class="bg-red-fill text-red p-4 rounded">bg-red-fill</div></div>
</div>
</Example>

Note the order of the words in the names: `--tblr-fill-primary` is the strongest neutral fill, while `--tblr-primary-fill` is the fill of the primary color.

## Gray palette

The gray scale is used for backgrounds, borders and muted text. Tabler ships several gray palettes and switches between them with `data-bs-theme-base`; see [theme base colors](/ui/plugins/theme-base-colors).

<Colors colors={site.grayColors} />

### Gray utilities

Every shade of the gray palette has a background and a text utility, from `50` to `950`. Use `bg-gray-*` for surfaces and `text-gray-*` for muted text, and pick a pair with enough contrast between them.

<Example>
<div class="bg-gray-100 text-gray-700 p-3 rounded mb-2">Light surface with dark text</div>
<div class="bg-gray-800 text-gray-100 p-3 rounded mb-2">Dark surface with light text</div>
<p class="text-gray-500 mb-0">Muted paragraph with text-gray-500</p>
</Example>

## Accessibility

- A color name is not a meaning. `text-red` looks like an error to most users, but a screen reader announces nothing, so keep the meaning in the text.
- Body text needs a contrast ratio of at least 4.5:1, and large text at least 3:1. The lighter `-lt` variants are backgrounds for dark text, not text colors.
- Check both color modes. A pair that passes in light can fail in dark, and Tabler switches many tokens with `light-dark()`.
- The social colors are brand colors. They are picked to be recognizable, not readable - do not use them for text on a white background without checking.
- Never separate two states by hue alone. Add a label, an icon, or a shape.
